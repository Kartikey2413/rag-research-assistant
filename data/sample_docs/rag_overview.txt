Retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG, is a way of answering questions by
combining two systems instead of relying on one. A retriever first searches
a collection of documents and pulls out the passages most relevant to the
question. A language model then reads those passages and writes an answer
grounded in them, rather than relying only on what it memorized during
training.

This matters for two reasons. First, it lets a model answer questions about
information it was never trained on, such as a company's internal
documents or a student's own notes, as long as those documents are indexed.
Second, it makes answers checkable: because the system knows which
passages it used, it can cite them, so a user can verify a claim instead of
taking it on faith.

A typical RAG pipeline has three stages: chunking documents into small
overlapping pieces, embedding or indexing those pieces so they can be
searched by meaning, and generating an answer conditioned on the
highest-scoring pieces for a given query.

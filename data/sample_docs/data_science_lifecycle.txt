The data science lifecycle describes the stages a project typically moves
through, from a business question to a working system. Problem framing
comes first: turning a vague goal into a specific, measurable question.
Data collection and cleaning follow, since most real-world data arrives
incomplete, duplicated, or inconsistently labeled.

Once the data is usable, feature engineering and modeling begin. This is
where raw fields become the inputs a model can learn from, and where a
baseline model is built before anything more complex is attempted.
Evaluation comes next: checking not just accuracy, but whether the model's
mistakes are the kind the business can tolerate.

The final and most often skipped stage is deployment and monitoring.
A model that only exists in a notebook creates no value; it needs to run
where decisions are actually made, and it needs to be watched afterward,
since real-world data drifts and a model's performance can quietly decay.

Transformers are a neural network architecture built around a mechanism
called self-attention, which lets every position in a sequence look at
every other position and decide how much to weigh it. This replaced
earlier approaches that processed text one step at a time, and it is the
architecture behind most modern large language models.

Each transformer layer has two main parts: a self-attention block, which
mixes information across positions, and a feed-forward block, which
transforms each position's representation independently. Stacking many of
these layers lets the network build up increasingly abstract
representations of the input.

Because attention has no built-in sense of order, transformers add
positional information separately, so the model can tell "the cat sat on
the mat" apart from "the mat sat on the cat." This combination of
attention plus position information is what allows transformers to scale
well to long sequences and large datasets.
